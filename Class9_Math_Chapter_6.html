EXERCISE SET 6.1 — Circumference & Arc Length

Question 1

The perimeter of a circle is 44 cm. What is its radius?

Solution

Given circumference \(C = 44\text{ cm}\). Formula: \(C = 2\pi r\).

\(2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times r = 44 \implies \frac{44}{7} r = 44 \implies r = 7\text{ cm}\).

Answer: The radius of the circle is 7 cm.

Question 2

Calculate, correct to 3 significant figures, the circumference of a circle with:

(i) radius 7 cm (ii) radius 10 cm (iii) radius 12 cm

Solution

(i) \(r = 7\text{ cm} \implies C = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 7 = 44.0\text{ cm}. (ii) \)r = 10\text{ cm} \implies C = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 10 = \frac{440}{7} \approx 62.9\text{ cm}.

(iii) \(r = 12\text{ cm} \implies C = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 12 = \frac{528}{7} \approx 75.4\text{ cm}. Question 3 Calculate the length of the arc of a circle if: (i) the radius is 3.5 cm and the angle at the centre is 60° (ii) the radius is 6.3 m and the angle at the centre is 120° Solution: Arc length formula \)l = 2\pi r \times \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\(. (i) \)l = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times \frac{60}{360} = 22 \times 0.5 \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{11}{3} \approx

3.67\text{ cm}.

(ii) \(l = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 6.3 \times \frac{120}{360} = 44 \times 0.9 \times \frac{1}{3} = 13.2\text{ m}. Question 4 Find the perimeter of a sector (the curved portion as well as the two straight portions) of a circle of radius 14 cm and sector angle 75°. Solution: Arc length \)l = 2\pi r \times \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 14 \times \frac{75}{360} = 88 \times

\frac{5}{24} = \frac{55}{3} \approx 18.33\text{ cm}\(. Perimeter of sector \)= l + 2r = 18.33 + 2(14) = 18.33 + 28 = 46.33\text{ cm} (or \(\frac{139}{3}\text{ cm}\)).

EXERCISE SET 6.2 — Area of Polygons & Heron's Formula Question 1 Find the area of triangle ADE in Fig. 6.31 (base DE = 10 cm, height = 8 cm). Solution: \)\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 8 = 40\text{ cm}^2.

Question 2

The parallel sides of a trapezium are 40 cm and 20 cm. If its non-parallel sides are both equal, each being 26 cm,

find the area of the trapezium.

Solution

Base projection \(x = \frac{40 - 20}{2} = 10\text{ cm}\).

Height \(h = \sqrt{26^2 - 10^2} = \sqrt{676 - 100} = \sqrt{576} = 24\text{ cm}\).

\(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h = \frac{1}{2}(40 + 20) \times 24 = 30 \times 24 = 720\text{ cm}^2. Question 3 Find the area of a triangle, given that its sides are 8 cm and 11 cm long, and its perimeter is 32 cm. Solution: Third side \)c = 32 - (8 + 11) = 13\text{ cm}\(. Semi-perimeter \)s = \frac{32}{2} = 16\text{ cm}\(. Heron's Formula: \)\text{Area} = \sqrt{16(16-8)(16-11)(16-13)} = \sqrt{16 \times 8 \times 5 \times 3} = \sqrt{1920} =

8\sqrt{30} \approx 43.82\text{ cm}^2.

Question 4

The sides of a triangular plot are in the ratio 3 : 5 : 7; its perimeter is 300 m. Find its area.

Solution

Let sides be \(3x, 5x, 7x\). \(3x + 5x + 7x = 300 \implies 15x = 300 \implies x = 20\text{ m}\).

Sides: \(a = 60\text{ m}, b = 100\text{ m}, c = 140\text{ m}\). \(s = 150\text{ m}\).

\(\text{Area} = \sqrt{150(150-60)(150-100)(150-140)} = \sqrt{150 \times 90 \times 50 \times 10} = \sqrt{6750000} = 1500\sqrt{3} \approx 2598.08\text{ m}^2. Question 5 One diagonal of a rhombus is twice as long as the other diagonal. If the rhombus has area 128 cm², find the length of the shorter diagonal. Solution: Let shorter diagonal be \)d_1\(, then \)d_2 = 2d_1\(. \)\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} d_1 d_2 = \frac{1}{2} d_1 (2d_1) = d_1^2 = 128 \implies d_1 = \sqrt{128} = 8\sqrt{2}

\approx 11.31\text{ cm}.

Question 6

ABCD is a parallelogram. P and Q are any two points on side AB. What can you say about the ratio area(ΔPCD) :

area(ΔQCD)?

Solution

Both \(\Delta PCD\) and \(\Delta QCD\) lie on the same base \(CD\) and between the same parallel lines \(AB \parallel CD\). Thus they have identical height \(h\).

\(\text{Area}(\Delta PCD) = \frac{1}{2} CD \cdot h = \text{Area}(\Delta QCD)\). Ratio is 1 : 1.

Question 7

O is any point on the diagonal PR of a parallelogram PQRS. Prove that the areas of triangles PSO and PQO are

equal.

Solution

Diagonal \(PR\) divides \(PQRS\) into two equal areas: \(\text{Area}(\Delta PQR) = \text{Area}(\Delta PSR)\).

Similarly, \(RO\) divides \(\Delta SQR\) into equal areas: \(\text{Area}(\Delta PRO) = \text{Area}(\Delta PRO)\).

Subtracting gives: \(\text{Area}(\Delta PSO) = \text{Area}(\Delta PQO)\). (Proved)

Question 8

If the mid-points of the sides of a 4-gon (quadrilateral) are joined in order, prove that the area of the parallelogram

thus formed will be half of the area of the given 4-gon.

Solution

By Varignon's Theorem, joining midpoints \(E, F, G, H\) creates a parallelogram.

The 4 corner triangles removed have areas equal to \(\frac{1}{4}\) of the corresponding diagonal triangles. Their

sum equals \(\frac{1}{2} \text{Area}(ABCD)\).

Hence, \(\text{Area}(EFGH) = \frac{1}{2} \text{Area}(ABCD)\). (Proved)

Question 9

In ΔABC, the midpoint of BC is D. Median AD is drawn. P is any point on AD. Show that area(ΔABP) = area(ΔACP).

Solution

Median \(AD \implies \text{Area}(\Delta ABD) = \text{Area}(\Delta ACD)\).

Median \(PD \text{ in } \Delta PBC \implies \text{Area}(\Delta PBD) = \text{Area}(\Delta PCD)\).

Subtracting equations: \(\text{Area}(\Delta ABP) = \text{Area}(\Delta ACP)\). (Proved)

Question 10

Given a square ABCD, let P be a point within it. Join PA, PB, PC, PD. What is the ratio of the areas of the red

region (ΔPAB + ΔPCD) and the green region (ΔPBC + ΔPDA)?

Solution

Let square side be \(s\). \(\text{Area}(\Delta PAB) + \text{Area}(\Delta PCD) = \frac{1}{2} s h_1 + \frac{1}{2} s h_2 = \frac{1}{2} s (h_1 + h_2) = \frac{1}{2} s^2\).

The remaining green region also equals \(\frac{1}{2} s^2\). Ratio is 1 : 1.

Question 11

In ΔABC, D is the midpoint of AB. P is any point on BC, and Q is a point on AB such that CQ || PD. Prove that

Area(ΔBPQ) = 1/2 Area(ΔABC).

Solution

Since \(CQ \parallel PD\), \(\text{Area}(\Delta PDC) = \text{Area}(\Delta PDQ) \implies \text{Area}(\Delta BPQ) = \text{Area}(\Delta BDC)\).

Since \(D\) is the midpoint of \(AB\), \(CD\) is a median \(\implies \text{Area}(\Delta BDC) = \frac{1}{2} \text{Area}(\Delta ABC)\).

Thus, \(\text{Area}(\Delta BPQ) = \frac{1}{2} \text{Area}(\Delta ABC)\). (Proved)

EXERCISE SET 6.3 — Area of Circle Sectors & Segments

Question 1

Find the area of a sector of a circle with radius 7 cm if the angle of the sector is 60°.

Solution

\(\text{Area} = \pi r^2 \times \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} = \frac{22}{7} \times 49 \times \frac{60}{360} = \frac{154}{6} = \frac{77}{3} \approx 25.67\text{ cm}^2. Question 2 Find the area of a quadrant of a circle whose circumference is 44 cm. Solution: \)2\pi r = 44 \implies r = 7\text{ cm}\(. \)\text{Quadrant Area} = \frac{1}{4} \pi r^2 = \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 49 = \frac{154}{4} = 38.5\text{

cm}^2.

Question 3

The length of the minute hand of a clock is 7 cm. Find the area swept by the minute hand in 10 minutes.

Solution

Angle swept in \(10\text{ min} = 10 \times 6^\circ = 60^\circ\).

\(\text{Area} = \frac{60}{360} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 49 = \frac{77}{3} \approx 25.67\text{ cm}^2. Question 4 A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends 90° at the centre. Find the area of the corresponding: (i) minor sector, and (ii) major sector. (Use π ≈ 3.14) Solution: (i) Minor sector area \)= \frac{90}{360} \times 3.14 \times 100 = 0.25 \times 314 = 78.5\text{ cm}^2.

(ii) Major sector area \(= (3.14 \times 100) - 78.5 = 314 - 78.5 = 235.5\text{ cm}^2. Question 5 A chord of a circle of radius 15 cm subtends 60° at the centre. Find the areas of corresponding minor and major segments. (Use π ≈ 3.14, √3 ≈ 1.73) Solution: Sector area \)= \frac{60}{360} \times 3.14 \times 225 = 117.75\text{ cm}^2\(. Triangle area \)= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} r^2 = \frac{1.73}{4} \times 225 = 97.3125\text{ cm}^2\(. • Minor segment \)= 117.75 - 97.3125 = 20.44\text{ cm}^2.

• Major segment \(= 706.5 - 20.44 = 686.06\text{ cm}^2. Question 6 A car has two wipers (blade 28 cm, sweeps 120°). Find total area cleaned at each sweep. Solution: \)\text{Total Area} = 2 \times \left( \frac{120}{360} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 28^2 \right) = 2 \times \frac{2464}{3} =

\frac{4928}{3} \approx 1642.67\text{ cm}^2.

Question 7

A chord of radius r subtends 60° at centre. Show that minor segment area = πr²(1/6 - √3/4π).

Solution

Sector area \(= \frac{1}{6} \pi r^2\). Equilateral triangle area \(= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} r^2\).

\(\text{Segment Area} = \frac{1}{6} \pi r^2 - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} r^2 = \pi r^2 \left( \frac{1}{6} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4\pi} \right)\). (Proved)

Question 8

Equilateral triangle inscribed in circle of radius r. Show ratio of area of triangle to circle = 3√3 / 4π ≈ 0.413.

Solution

Triangle side \(a = \sqrt{3} r \implies \text{Area} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(\sqrt{3}r)^2 = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4} r^2\). Circle area

\(= \pi r^2\).

\(\text{Ratio} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi} \approx 0.413\). (Proved)

Question 9

Square inscribed in circle of radius r. Show ratio of area of square to circle = 2 / π ≈ 0.637.

Solution

Diagonal \(= 2r \implies \text{Square side } a = \sqrt{2}r \implies \text{Area} = 2r^2\).

\(\text{Ratio} = \frac{2r^2}{\pi r^2} = \frac{2}{\pi} \approx 0.637\). (Proved)

Question 10

Hexagon inscribed in circle of radius r. Show ratio of area = 3√3 / 2π ≈ 0.827. Why is it twice Question 8?

Solution

Hexagon area \(= 6 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} r^2 = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} r^2\). Ratio \(= \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2\pi} \approx 0.827\).

It is exactly twice Question 8 because a regular hexagon inscribed in a circle is composed of 6 equilateral triangles,

whereas an inscribed triangle has only 3.

End-of-Chapter Q1

Draw figures corresponding to area identities \((a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2\) and \((a+b+c)^2 = a^2+b^2+c^2+2ab+2bc+2ca\).

Solution
• \((a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2\): A large square of side \(a\) with a corner \(b \times b\) removed leaves area \(a^2 - b^2\),

which cuts into a rectangle of length \((a+b)\) and width \((a-b)\).

• \((a+b+c)^2\): A square of side \((a+b+c)\) partitioned into 3 squares (\(a^2, b^2, c^2\)) and 6 rectangles (\(2ab, 2bc, 2ca\)).

End-of-Chapter Q2

An isosceles triangle has perimeter 40 cm; the equal sides are 15 cm each. Find the area of the triangle.

Solution

Base \(c = 40 - (15 + 15) = 10\text{ cm}\). Height \(h = \sqrt{15^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{200} = 10\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}\).

\(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 10\sqrt{2} = 50\sqrt{2} \approx 70.71\text{ cm}^2. End-of-Chapter Q3 An isosceles triangle has base 10 cm, and its area is 60 cm². End-of-Chapter Q10

Two rectangles have the same area and the same perimeter. Does this mean that they are congruent to each

other?

Solution

Let lengths be \(l, w\). \(l+w = S/2\) and \(lw = A\). \(l, w\) are the unique roots of quadratic \(t^2 - (S/2)t + A = 0\).

Answer: Yes, they must have identical dimensions and are therefore congruent.

All three geometric proofs demonstrate that \(\text{Area}(\text{Trapezium}) = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h\), where \(a, b\) are

End-of-Chapter Q14

Show that the area of a kite is half the product of its diagonals using algebra and geometry.

Solution
• Algebraic: Diagonals \(d_1, d_2\) intersect at \(90^\circ\). \(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} d_1 x + \frac{1}{2} d_1 (d_2-x) = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2} d_1 d_2}\).
• Geometric: Enclose kite in rectangle of \(d_1 \times d_2\); kite area equals half the bounding rectangle.

End-of-Chapter Q15

Three problems about fitting congruent shapes (scaling factor \(k\)):

Solution

Scaling side lengths by \(k\) scales area by \(k^2\).

(i) Scale 2x \(\implies\) Area 4x. Yes, 4 copies fit.

(ii) Triangle scale 2x \(\implies\) Area 4x. Yes, 4 copies fit.

(iii) Triangle scale 3x \(\implies\) Area 9x. Yes, 9 copies fit.
