1. Chapter Summary: Indian Election System & Democracy

A. Introduction to Elections and Democratic Necessity

An election is a formal democratic process through which citizens exercise their right to vote and choose their representatives to public office. India is a representative democracy and currently the most populous country in the world, making the conduct of regular elections a complex task.

Periodic elections form the core and heart of a democratic system. Without regular elections, the entire framework of governance and democracy becomes hollow. Elections ensure four fundamental democratic pillars:

  • Equality — Gives every citizen an equal voice through one vote.
  • Representation — Enables citizens to elect leaders who make laws and decisions on their behalf.
  • Accountability — Keeps elected representatives answerable to the people.
  • Legitimacy — Provides legal and moral authority to the elected government.
😄 Politics Joke: In India, elections are the only time when politicians remember your name, your gully, and even your dog’s name… after that they forget even their own promises!

B. Types of Elections in India

Direct Elections: Citizens vote directly. All citizens aged 18+ vote under Universal Adult Franchise. Held every 5 years for Lok Sabha, Vidhan Sabha, and Local Bodies.

Indirect Elections: Elected representatives vote. Used for President, Vice President, Rajya Sabha, and Vidhan Parishad.

C. Structure of Government and Legislature

Union Level: President + Lok Sabha (543 members) + Rajya Sabha (245 seats: 233 elected + 12 nominated).

State Level: Vidhan Sabha in all 28 states. Vidhan Parishad only in 6 bicameral states: Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh.

D. Electoral Systems

First-Past-The-Post (FPTP): Candidate with highest votes wins (no need for 50% majority). Used for Lok Sabha & Vidhan Sabha.

Proportional Representation with STV: Voters rank candidates. Candidate must reach a Quota. Used for Rajya Sabha, President, Vice President, Vidhan Parishad.

Quota Formula:
Quota = (Total Valid Votes ÷ (Seats to be Filled + 1)) + 1

E. Primary Electoral Laws & Delimitation

  • RPA 1950 — Pre-election work (seats, delimitation, electoral rolls).
  • RPA 1951 — Actual conduct of elections, corrupt practices, disputes.
  • Presidential & VP Elections Act, 1952
  • Delimitation Commission (Art. 82) — 1952, 1963, 1973, 2002.

F. Election Commission of India (ECI)

Autonomous body established on 25 January 1950 under Articles 324–329. Celebrated as National Voters’ Day.

Key functions: Electoral rolls & SIR, scheduling, party registration & symbols, enforcing MCC, digital apps, inclusive voting.

😄 Politics Joke: The Election Commission is the only organisation in India that can make a Chief Minister stand in a queue… and still smile for the camera.

G. Political Parties & Anti-Defection

India has a multi-party system. Recognition criteria for National and State parties are based on vote share and seats.

Anti-Defection Law (52nd Amendment, 1985): Prevents elected members from switching parties. Disqualification decided by Speaker/Chairman.

H. Challenges to Free & Fair Elections

  • Misinformation & fake news
  • Money power & bribes
  • Voter intimidation & booth capturing
  • Divisive appeals (religion, caste, language)

ECI counters these with MCC, cVIGIL, EVMs + VVPAT, and strict enforcement of RPA.

4. 10 Short-Answer Questions + Answers

Q1. What is Psephology?
Scientific study of elections and voting patterns (from Greek ‘psēphos’ = pebble).

Q2. Difference between Direct and Indirect elections?
Direct: Citizens vote themselves (Lok Sabha, Vidhan Sabha).
Indirect: Elected representatives vote (President, Rajya Sabha).

Q3. What is FPTP?
Candidate with highest votes in a constituency wins, even without 50% majority.

Q4. Quota formula in PR-STV?
Quota = (Total Valid Votes ÷ (Seats + 1)) + 1

Q5. Six bicameral states?
Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh.

Q6. RPA 1950 vs RPA 1951?
1950 = preparation (rolls, seats). 1951 = conduct of elections & offences.

Q7. What is SIR?
Special Intensive Revision — cleaning and updating electoral rolls.

Q8. What is Defection & which law deals with it?
Switching party after election. Anti-Defection Law (52nd Amendment, 1985).

Q9. cVIGIL and Suvidha apps?
cVIGIL = report MCC violations. Suvidha = campaign permissions.

Q10. Special voting in 2024?
Home voting for senior citizens (85+) and PwDs (40%+ disability).
